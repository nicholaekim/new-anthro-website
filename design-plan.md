## 1. Cinematic storytelling

### Story spine

| Chapter | Place | Historical date | Present-day date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Louisiana, United States | 1930 | 2020 |
| 2 | São Paulo, Brazil | 1950 | 2020 |
| 3 | Johannesburg, South Africa | 1955 | 2020 |

The default order is the one in your brief. A visitor may arrive at any chapter first, so no chapter should depend on another.

### The person card

- It holds [the facts you define about the fictional person] and [your visible statement that the person is fictional].
- It never moves after the page loads. Only a highlight on its lines changes.
- It carries no physical description, and each fact has [your sourced reason for including it]. Open question: [how you will handle a source whose basis involves appearance, without describing or depicting a body].

### Staging on desktop

- **One viewport composition, used on every chapter page.** A narrow column reserved for the person card and the fixed controls, which section content never enters. An item area. A reading column.
- **One backdrop per chapter page.** It holds the surface, the light and the person card. The light belongs to the backdrop only and never covers anything in a section.
- **Beats are sibling sections in page order** that scroll over the backdrop. The chapter card, the place photograph and the closing section are opaque, so they hide the backdrop. Their edges simply scroll past. The black of the chapter card is what separates chapters.
- **Two sections pin, for about one screen each:** the place photograph and the change of date. They are siblings and never sit inside one another.
- **Items belong to their own section,** so they scroll away by themselves.

### Staging on phones

No backdrop and no pins. The page is the still version in a single column, with short fades as sections arrive. The person card becomes a fixed button above the content that opens the card, placed so it never covers the element that has keyboard focus.

### Before you use the skeleton

First describe, for each place and date, how classification operated according to your sources. The skeleton below assumes four things: a written basis, a body that records, an instrument, and an outcome recorded for an individual. Where an assumption does not hold for a place or date, drop or replace that beat.

Also decide how alike the four chapters should look, because either choice says something:

| Option | What it implies |
|---|---|
| Uniform treatment: the same light, framing and pacing in every chapter | That the four cases can be set side by side |
| Differentiated treatment: each chapter's treatment follows from its sources | That they cannot be assumed comparable. Each departure needs [its justification and source]. |

### Chapter introductions: three options

1. A black chapter card with place and date, then the photograph. Cheapest.
2. A decorative date line runs back from the previous chapter's present-day date to this chapter's historical date before the card appears.
3. The photograph is present from the start and the place and date sit in the dark area beside it.

### Opening route

Purpose: establish the person card and the fiction, and give the visitor [your site-level note on language and content] before any source material.

### Ending route

Decide first: [your conclusion on whether and how the four cases can be compared]. The layout follows from it. If they can be set side by side, show the records together. If not, present them in sequence. Then [your reflection on what the site asked the visitor to do], your method note, note on language, bibliography, image credits, the statement of what is fictional or reconstructed, and your AI-use disclosure. ### Layers and pacing

- In beat 2, depth comes from two or three foreground shapes you make yourself and reuse in every chapter. They move slightly as the visitor scrolls. The photograph does not move. You may sample colours from each photograph, but the shapes are your own material.
- Put the quietest moment directly before each change of date.
- The weightiest moment gets the least motion.
- Give all four places equal time for evidence and analysis.
- No flicker, shake, bounce, glitch, light leaks or scratches.

Two moments are worth your polish: beat 2 and beat 6. Everything else can be plain.

## 2. GSAP techniques

Each block gives what the feature is, where this design uses it, and the warning that matters most. Ask your instructor whether every technique you list has to be demonstrated in the build, because several are marked as extras here.

**Scroll-driven storytelling**
- What: ScrollTrigger connects a timeline to scroll. `pin` holds a section and `scrub` ties progress to the scrollbar. A numeric scrub is the seconds the playhead takes to catch up, which smooths it. `snap` can settle on timeline labels after the visitor stops, and gives way if they scroll again.
- Where: beats 2 and 6 on desktop. Snap is an extra.
- Warning: with scrub, durations are proportions of the scroll distance, not seconds.

**Parallax backgrounds**
- What: scrubbed tweens on transform that move layers by different amounts over the same scroll distance.
- Where: beat 2 only, on shapes you made.
- Warning: give each source of movement its own wrapper. Two tweens on the same element's transform overwrite each other. `gsap.quickTo()` exists for pointer-driven drift, which is an extra.

**Historical-to-modern transitions**
- What: two layers at the same position, cross-faded with opacity on one pinned, scrubbed timeline.
- Where: beat 6, between the historical item's mount and the present-day item's mount. The person card is in the backdrop and is not part of the animation, which is why it reads as constant.
- Warning: do not animate blur, filters or gradients. An outline of the earlier mount left faintly visible afterwards is an extra, and must be decoration only. A hand-driven version is also an extra: a native range slider can drive a paused timeline (read about `gsap.utils.mapRange()`).

**Animated timelines**
- What: a line that grows between two dates. A bar scaled along its length with a transform needs no plugin. DrawSVGPlugin draws a stroked line progressively if you want a drawn look.
- Where: the decorative date line in beat 6. It is separate from the place-and-date tag in the interface and is hidden from assistive technology. If your research gives you [dated points in between], list them as ordinary buttons in page order.
- Warning: DrawSVG needs a stroke. `gsap.utils.snap()` rounds a tweened number to whole years.

**Interactive documents**
- What: the Flip plugin records where an element is, lets the layout change, then animates from the old position to the new one.
- Where: an extra. By default an item is a small image, and its close view is a separate large image in a dialog.
- Warning: in React the layout change happens on a later render, and the animation has to start after it. The Flip page covers this, and its way of pairing two different elements. Flip does not handle 3D transforms. Keeping it out of scrubbed scenes is this plan's advice.

**Chapter transitions**
- What: each chapter begins and ends on black, so a plain route change already reads as a cut.
- Where: a plain route change is the default. A cover layer that closes before the route changes is an extra.
- Warning: the App Router has no hook that waits for a scripted exit animation. The Link component documents an onNavigate handler (Next.js 15.3 and later) that can cancel a navigation, but it covers Link clicks only. Back, Forward and other navigation cut straight through. Next.js also documents declarative transitions through React's ViewTransition, placed in each page, not the shared layout. That guide assumes recent versions, so check yours.

**Text and typography**
- What: SplitText breaks text into lines, words or characters. `mask` clips lines for a rising reveal. `autoSplit` re-splits when fonts load or the width changes. CustomEase defines your own curve. ScrollTrigger's `toggleActions` sets what happens on entering and leaving, and its default plays on first arrival and does not reset.
- Where: the first extra to add, on the title and chapter place and date only.
- Warning: SplitText's default labels the split element and hides the pieces from screen readers. That works on a heading without links. For other text, the SplitText and GSAP accessibility pages describe an alternative, and either way test with a screen reader. With autoSplit, the reveal has to be built inside the `onSplit` callback, or it keeps pointing at pieces that were discarded. Body text is simply present, and words from sources are never animated letter by letter.

### Rules that hold the build together

- **One timeline, one trigger.** Put the trigger on the parent timeline, not on tweens inside it. Otherwise two things drive the same playhead.
- **Scrub or toggleActions, not both, on one trigger.** A GSAP forum administrator wrote that they are mutually exclusive "as far as I know" (gsap.com/community/forums/topic/33904, read 2026-11-23). The docs do not say so directly.
- **One input per timeline.** If scroll drives a timeline, nothing else sets its progress, because the next scroll movement overrides it. So on desktop the era switch moves the page between the start and end of beat 6, which the trigger already knows. On phones, where beat 6 is not scrubbed, the switch plays a timed cross-fade in place. The page's era comes from scroll position alone.
- **React.** The `useGSAP()` hook comes from the separate @gsap/react package. It limits selectors to one component only when given a scope. Without one, a selector matches every copy of a repeated component. It reverts what was created while it ran when the component unmounts. Handlers that start animations later need `contextSafe`, and a change in dependencies reverts nothing unless `revertOnUpdate` is on.
- **Next.js.** Animated pieces are client components. React strict mode runs set-up twice in development, which the hook's clean-up absorbs.
- **Responsive and reduced motion.** `gsap.matchMedia()` is part of the core. It runs a set-up while a media query matches and reverts it when it stops matching. A visible motion switch is your own setting, not a media query. The `gsap.matchMediaRefresh()` page describes exactly that case.
- **Back, Forward and reload.** Decide the behaviour and test it once pins exist. Starting at the top of the chapter is the simple choice, and ScrollTrigger documents a way to clear remembered scroll positions.
- **Debugging.** `markers` shows each trigger's start and end. GSDevTools does not work with scroll-driven animations, so get each timeline working on a clock first.

### Pitfalls stated in the GSAP docs

- Do not animate the pinned element itself. Pin a plain wrapper and move something inside it.
- Nested pinning is not supported.
- Create triggers in page order.
- An ancestor with a transform breaks pinning.
- Positions are measured at creation and on refresh, so refresh after late content loads. The SplitText page says fonts loading late shift layout.
- Native smooth scrolling should be off wherever GSAP controls scrolling, and CSS transitions should not sit on properties GSAP animates.
- ScrollTrigger has a setting, `ignoreMobileResize`, for the phone address bar. `ScrollTrigger.normalizeScroll()` is documented as experimental.
- The `once` option removes a trigger after it fires, which also unpins it, so it does not suit a pinned section.

This plan's own advice, not GSAP's: give every image its dimensions so nothing shifts after measuring, and if you size full-height scenes with small-viewport units, check on a phone what shows when the address bar retracts.

| When | Pieces | Note |
|---|---|---|

| First extra | SplitText | Title and chapter-card reveals |

MotionPath's alignment is calculated once and not re-applied on resize. ScrambleText and TextPlugin give no screen-reader guidance. None has a job here. |

## 4. Visual design

### Light and atmospheric backgrounds

| Layer | Contribution | Options |
|---|---|---|

| Surface | Material and weight | Your own photograph of a plain surface, or a plain dark field if you want less staging |

## 5. User experience and navigation

### Structure

Six routes under one shared layout: opening, four chapters, ending. The navigation menu lists all six, with each chapter named by place and both dates.

### Getting around

- Linear by default, with the menu always reachable from one button.
- The place-and-date tag changes only at beat boundaries.
- Leave arrow, space and page keys doing what the browser does.
- After a chapter change, move keyboard focus to the new chapter's heading.

### Desktop and mobile

Use one agreed width as the breakpoint for both layout (Tailwind) and animation (the matchMedia condition).

| | Desktop | Phone |
|---|---|---|
| Layout | The three-area composition over the backdrop | One column, no backdrop |
| Pinning | Beats 2 and 6, about one screen each | None |
| Change of date | Scrubbed cross-fade, plus the era switch | Timed cross-fade from the era switch, both states also readable in sequence |
| Documents | Close view beside the transcription | Full-screen sheet, transcription first, native pinch-zoom on the scan |
| Person card | In its reserved column | A fixed button that opens it |
| Pointer effects | Extras only | None |

Tailwind notes: define the palette roles as named theme colours. Tailwind has variants for the reduced-motion preference and utilities for small-viewport heights. Do not put its transition utilities on anything GSAP animates. Each document has a small image for the page and a large one that loads only when the close view opens.

### Reduced motion and the still version

Build the still version first, with every slot present. It is the reduced-motion version and a plain reading route. Honour the system preference and offer a visible motion switch. Test: anything learnable in the animated version is learnable in the still version, in the same order.

Without scripts, the text, images, disclosures and links still work, and the dialog, era switch and motion switch do not. Make sure both eras and every item are readable in sequence in that case.

Content is visible by default and hidden states are applied by the animation set-up. For anything at the top of a page, the default here is to leave it un-animated. GSAP's page on flashes of unstyled content describes the alternative.

### Accessibility checklist

- Targets at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels (WCAG 2.5.8, level AA). This plan adopts 44 by 44 on touch, the figure from 2.5.5 (level AAA).
- Anything draggable also works without dragging (WCAG 2.5.7).
- Text contrast of 4.5 to 1, or 3 to 1 for large text (WCAG 1.4.3), measured at the least favourable spot the text can sit on: the lightest for light text, the darkest for dark text (W3C failure technique F83).
- A visible focus indicator (2.4.7) that is never entirely hidden behind a fixed or pinned layer (2.4.11), with 3 to 1 contrast on it and on meaningful indicators (1.4.11).
- No loss of content and no sideways scrolling at 320 CSS pixels wide (1.4.10). Document scans are the exception, which is another reason for transcriptions.
- Motion that starts by itself, lasts more than five seconds and runs beside other content needs a way to pause, stop or hide it (2.2.2).
- Outcomes are ordinary text under a heading in reading order. Decorative layers are hidden from assistive technology.

### Performance checklist

- Animate transforms and opacity only.
- The first image on each page is a real image, loaded eagerly, visible without script. The setting's name depends on your Next.js version.
- Give every image its dimensions and a sizes hint.
- Write per-frame values straight to the element, not through React state, which would re-render the component on every frame.

## 6. Build order

One reviewer estimated the build work in the previous draft at 70 to 80 hours, against the 48 to 60 that six weeks at eight to ten hours provides. That is an estimate, not a measurement. The default below has already been cut in response: no prediction, no pinned outcome, still opening and ending. Research, sourcing, asset making and writing are on top of the build hours, so give them their own weekly time.

**First days, before any build**

- Send your instructor: this plan, the AI-use question, the Tobique questions, any accessibility expectation, whether listed techniques must be demonstrated, and what form the submission takes.
- Ask the library's copyright office whether a public course site is covered by an educational exception.
- Send archive permission requests. They have the longest lead time.
- Answer the two questions under the directions. Decide the chapter order and where the site is hosted.

**Research and sourcing track, every week, with its own hours**

- By the end of week 1: notes on how classification operated in all four places at both dates, and the departures each chapter needs from the skeleton.
- By the end of week 2: the Tobique gate date.
- Throughout: the ledger, and a transcription and two descriptions for each item as it clears. The visual-choice table before animating.

**Build track**

| Week | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | Project set-up and first publish, so a phone can reach it. Typefaces loaded. One chapter as a still page in grey boxes with every slot, controls as inert placeholders, on desktop and phone. Learn tweens and timelines. |
| 2 | Walk all four chapters through the content file on paper, then fix its shape. Beat 2 pinned and scrubbed with debug markers. Beat 6 with the era switch, on desktop and phone. |
| 3 | The other three chapters as content, with each real asset brought in as it clears. The close-view dialog. Provenance and citation disclosures. Menu, ticks, previous and next. |

**Assets for a complete build, about 20:** 12 to 16 sourced (per place a photograph, one or two historical items, a present-day item) and about six you make (surface, person card, your sheet stock, reserved-colour shape, two or three foreground shapes).

**If you have four weeks:** one historical item per place, no annotations, no foreground shapes, and beat 6 as the only animated beat in all four chapters. Weeks 1 and 2 merge, and weeks 5 and 6 merge.

## 7. Cut list

Extras were never scheduled, so skipping them costs nothing: split-text reveals, snap, pointer drift, an animated opening, an outline of the earlier mount, a hand-driven change of date, a travelling close view, a drawn line, hotspots on images, a cover between routes, the prediction.

Real cuts, in order:

1. Foreground shapes in beat 2. It becomes an ordinary section, and the site no longer has parallax.
2. Annotations on items. Keep transcription and provenance.
3. The scrubbed change of date. Use the timed cross-fade from the era switch everywhere.
4. The close-view dialog. Link to the large image and keep the transcription on the page.
5. The motion switch. Rely on the system preference.

Never cut: provenance, the three status labels, transcriptions, content notes, the still version, keyboard access, credits and disclosure.

## 8. Decisions that are yours

1. **Whether this plan is within what your course allows,** and what disclosure is required.
2. **Which direction.** The surface, another of the four, or your own.
3. **The two questions under the directions.**
4. **How alike the four chapters look.** Uniform or differentiated.
5. **The constant.** A person card, or a figure you draw. A figure, however neutral, carries cues of body, hair and dress.
6. **The prediction.** Keep it, limit it to one, or leave it out.
7. **Whether any outcome is emphasised,** and why.
8. **A view that changes shape between dates.** A narrow view for the past that opens wide for the present suggests something has lifted. The default here is one view for both.
9. **The Tobique chapter.** See section 3.
10. **Practical questions.** Chapter order, hosting, submission format, any accessibility expectation, whether listed techniques must be demonstrated, and the copyright position of a public site.
